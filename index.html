SEN-01

Wearable Pressure Sensor

Developed a MEMS microdome pressure sensor that remains sensitive while maintaining response under high loads, as part of an industry-partnered development program.

Engineering Challenge

Compliant structures respond well at low pressure but tend to saturate early, while stiffer structures sacrifice low-pressure resolution. The device also needed to remain durable and manufacturable within a cleanroom-compatible process.

What I Did

Designed and optimized the microdome geometry using contact mechanics and FEA to extend the working range while preserving low-pressure sensitivity. Developed and characterized the cleanroom fabrication process, including photolithography, isotropic RIE, and metal deposition, and established a stable process window for dome formation. Built a custom mechanical test setup and qualification workflow to evaluate sensitivity, range, hysteresis, durability, and environmental effects. During production scale-up, investigated anomalous sensor performance, traced the issue to a supplier-side fabrication defect, and implemented a design change that preserved the required performance.

Skills Demonstrated

MEMS device design Contact-mechanics-driven geometry optimization FEA study definition Cleanroom microfabrication (photolithography, RIE, e-beam evaporation) Process-window characterization Custom test-fixture design Root-cause defect investigation Vendor / industry-partner collaboration Technical documentation

Validation / Outcome

  • 3 MPa working range with 1,000-cycle durability
  • Multi-metric qualification: sensitivity, linearity, hysteresis, dynamic range, durability, noise, resolution, and temperature/humidity effects
  • Supplier-side defect resolved without compromising specification or schedule

SEN-02

Auxetic / Kirigami Optical Strain Sensor

Developed a wearable optical strain-sensing platform using auxetic and kirigami structures to improve strain uniformity, sensitivity, and working range.

Engineering Challenge

Wearable strain sensors need to maintain sensitivity over large deformation while remaining compliant and compact. Conventional structures often develop localized strain, limiting usable range and signal consistency.

What I Did

Designed auxetic and kirigami geometries in SolidWorks to control strain distribution and optical response under mechanical, fabrication, and form-factor constraints. Integrated the sensor with readout electronics, BLE communication, and a mobile interface across multiple prototype generations. Extended the design to kirigami structures for larger strain range and supported later collaborative model-driven optimization of the geometry. Led a Unity-based AR demonstration for real-time motion visualization.

Skills Demonstrated

Wearable sensor design Auxetic / kirigami structures Optomechanical sensing SolidWorks Prototype integration BLE sensor systems Readout electronics System-level design AR / Unity demonstration

Validation / Outcome

  • Achieved a 0–80% strain working range using kirigami structural design
  • Improved strain-distribution uniformity through auxetic geometry
  • Demonstrated integrated sensing, wireless readout, and real-time motion visualization
  • Extended the platform from auxetic sensing to kirigami structures and subsequent collaborative model-driven optimization

SYS-01

Instrumented Binder-Jet Platform

Upgraded an unreliable custom binder-jet printer into a repeatable, instrumented platform for automated layer-wise experimentation.

Engineering Challenge

The existing platform suffered from intermittent build failures, unsynchronized image acquisition, and motion-related surface defects, making repeatable process studies difficult.

What I Did

Improved system reliability by debugging and integrating the motion, inkjet, imaging, and illumination subsystems through a Python-based control layer. Built an in-situ imaging system with synchronized layer-by-layer acquisition and calibration, and corrected motion-sequence issues that were introducing repeatable surface defects. Developed an automated parameter-study workflow with experiment logging, allowing repeatable DOE campaigns and consistent dataset generation.

Skills Demonstrated

System integration Camera and illumination integration Motion-control troubleshooting Serial communication Python automation DOE workflow development

Validation / Outcome

  • Improved reliability from approximately one-in-three successful batches to multi-hundred-layer builds
  • Eliminated recurring surface-streaking defects through motion-sequence correction
  • Generated consistent layer-synchronized image datasets for downstream analysis
  • Enabled independent operation by undergraduate researchers

About

I'm a systems integration engineer who builds intelligent physical systems by connecting hardware, software, sensing, and computer vision. My background is in mechanical engineering, sensor systems, and additive manufacturing, and I specialize in integrating physical equipment with software and data-driven monitoring tools to create functional manufacturing platforms.

A lot of my strongest work has started outside of what was formally assigned — prototyping things I thought were worth trying, seeing what worked and what didn't, and following the ones that held up. Right now, that means building the instrumented hardware and data platform — synchronized camera, illumination, and acquisition pipeline — that lets computer vision monitor a binder-jet printing process, laying the groundwork for future closed-loop, AI-driven process control.

My technical foundation is a PhD in Mechanical Engineering from KAIST and hands-on work across MEMS pressure sensors, CNT/elastomer flexible strain sensors, wearable systems, and piezoelectric metamaterials — including supporting an industry-partner development project with Samsung on vendor-facing troubleshooting and defect investigation. My toolset spans COMSOL/ANSYS FEA, SolidWorks, MATLAB/Python, LDV measurement, cleanroom microfabrication, and, more recently, Python-based systems development and computer vision integration.

In the long term, I want to bring AI-based decision-making and control into physical systems, particularly for autonomous closed-loop manufacturing.
